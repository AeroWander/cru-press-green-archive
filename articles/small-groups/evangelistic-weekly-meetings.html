One of the most effective means of evangelism on campus is for students to invite their friends to an evangelistic talk or event—provided, of course, that the event is done well. The problem is that it’s typically not in the campus budget to bring in an evangelistic speaker. The solution is to do it yourself. Take the points, content, and approaches used by evangelistic speakers and do the talk yourself. Show some interesting video clips, hijack their data and research, and follow the outline of their presentation. No, it will probably not be as good, but it is both cheap and effective.

Here are a handful of my evangelistic talk outlines, feel free to pillage. But if you don’t use these, still think in terms of doing a low-cost version of existing outreach topics and talks.

While this will save you some money, the bigger reason for this approach is so that you can have multiple evangelistic meetings over the course of the year. Ideally, every six weeks or so your meeting should be something that students can invite their non-Christian friends to.

As you’ll see from the following outlines, events on the calendar provide great opportunities for outreach topics.

The Apostle Paul makes a profound analogy that can get lost in his treatment of marriage. He likens a relationship with how we eat. I would suggest that what’s wrong with relationships can be best seen by surveying what’s wrong with how we eat.

Bulimic Relationships

In the disease of bulimia the person longs for the taste of food, but they don’t want what comes with it, weight, nutrition, and calories. So they have developed a gag reflex that allows them to taste the food while avoiding a commitment to its digestion.

Example: have you ever known someone that enjoys relationships, but when the conversation turns to talk of marriage and long term commitment, they say, “excuse me, I need to go to the bathroom, blechhhh.” They have a gag reflex to commitment. They love the taste of a relationship but there’s no way they’re going to swallow it.

Story: If you can think of one..

Anorexic Relationships

This is a serious illness where the person literally starves themselves, depriving themselves of all needed nutrition, to drain all life from the body.

Example: There are people, bless their souls, who were never loved as children, or whatever the reason, who are emotional black holes. They suck all light and life from the other person in the relationship. They manipulate the other person to sever their other close relational ties, and then once isolated, like a vampire suck all the life out of their partner. They are pathologically narcissistic.

Story: I had a friend in one of these relationships and you could see she had lost all her creativity, excitement, and life…

Vegans

Vegans are vegetarians but when they eat they are also making a political statement by their diet. Their eating has a social agenda.

Example: Some people date their way up the food chain, always thinking about how dating a particular person will change their social status.

Example: The show Hoe millionaire. Marrying for money, these vegans are exposed.

In Search of Satan

When the Exorcist was first released I was young a teenager; unlike other horror movies I remember the terror of this film, for me and many others, lay in the possibility that such things might actually happen. That was many years ago, since then I’ve gone to seminary and am now in the ministry. I recently

read that the Exorcist was re-released with added footage: with theological training behind me there sparked a desire to finally research this topic for myself. In my years in ministry I must admit that I have never witnessed anything resembling that which was portrayed in the Exorcist. So, with a little bit of cynicism I decided to research the topic. What follows is a brief summary of my investigation and conclusions.

The Real Exorcist

In 1994 Thomas Allen published a book entitled Possessed which chronicles his research of the documented 1949 exorcism that inspired the movie The Exorcist. The possessed child was actually a boy (his name remains anonymous), changed to a young girl named Reagen in the movie, to protect the privacy of the individual.)

Apparently the boy, as in the movie, and his aunt, a medium, had been regularly playing with a Ouija board, which soon brought an unwanted presence and total change of the child’s personality.

One night while his parents entertained company the boy got out of bed and came down to the living room. A vase began to levitate and was sent hurling toward a wall along with a piece of heavy furniture. That night as the boy slept his parents checked on him. They watched, as the appearance of scratches seemed to welt up on his body. They watched as a bloody scratch wrote on the boys’ stomach the word HELL.

He was taken to Georgetown hospital where a pediatrician told the parents “I can’t give you a natural explanation for this.” Eventually several Jesuits priests (6 in all) were sought in St. Louis to perform the exorcism which apparently lasted about 20 days. Allen, the author of Possessed, obtained the diary of Father Bowdern, one of the exorcists. You can read the first hand account yourself as Allen includes the diary in the appendix. Allen notes that the movie does a great deal of embellishing in the story line and occasionally adds events from other documented exorcism accounts. On a creepy endnote, prior to it’s demolition, the abandoned wing of the hospital where the exorcism was performed had been secretly utilized in recent years by a satanic group for rituals-however they found out it’s location. Allen’s research on the real “Exorcist” account was recently transformed into a movie for ShowTime.

Historical Analysis

It seems that in most of the horror movies that I’ve watched, someone uncovers some dusty old scroll that contains the secrets of possession and the hidden art of exorcism, apparently cloistered in some monastery for a millennia or two. So I surveyed of some of the ancient literature of the early Christian Church (c.100-400 AD) to see what I might uncover on the subject of demonic possession.

Methodius (c. 260 AD) seems to be the first to mention the practice of ongoing exorcisms by the Church. Tertullian (c. early third century) indicates that the “power (of exorcism) should be taken seriously and not exercised indiscriminately.” Saint Augustine notes that the binding of Satan was accomplished through Jesus’ invasion of Satan’s territory on earth, but that there were “continuing acts of binding (exorcism) that were still taking place.”

In summary the creepy old scrolls (actually they’re available in most libraries) tell us very little and give us no eyewitness accounts of possession or exorcism. What I did find of interest was that the practice of exorcism has changed very little in the last two thousand years, at least within the Catholic Church. The Roman Exorcism Ritual that is still used today, dates back to the second century and has not been significantly altered in 1,6000 years. I guess the position of the Church is “if it works don’t mess with it.”

Evil Alive and Well

With the advent of modern science, medicine and psychology I think most of us would have assumed that incidents of possession would have evaporated and would now be more accurately diagnosed or treated through medicine or psychology. This, however, does not seem to be the case.

I do not mean there aren’t thousands of cases that would have been thought to be demonic possession in previous centuries. For instance, Tourettes Syndrome or Schizophrenia may have been labeled in the past as demonic possession, but we now know that their causes are physiological, not spiritual. What I mean is that there have been a persistent number of cases of individuals who manifest behavior and observable phenomenon where the label “possession” seems to be an accurate description. The number of cases reported has actually continued to rise not only in more primitive countries but in the United States as well. The Catholic Church reports anywhere from 800

Mark chapter 5 it says that Jesus met a man with an evil spirit. The passage says that he “lived in the tombs (graveyard) and no one could bind him not even with a chain…no one was strong enough to subdue him. Night and day among the tombs…he would cry out and cut himself with stones.”

Some Causes

From my investigation it does not seem that a person

simply wakes up and finds themselves possessed. It would seem that an abundance of verified cases have the following root causes.

  1. Involvement in the occult. Usually there seems to be voluntary involvement or participation with the supernatural. For example the true historical case that was the basis for The Exorcist began with a young boys obsession with a Ouija Board.
  2. Extreme drug use. It is not an accident that the Greek word for sorcery is pharmakeia from which we get the word pharmacy. Drugs have been historically used in many forms of sorcery or magic to commune with the spirits. Perhaps certain drugs let down cognitive guards of the mind and provide more open access to the soul-I just don’t know.
  3. Ancestral involvement with the occult can also be traced as an efficient cause.

Besides these major gateways it is also observable that phenomenon associated with “possession” has occurred in individuals through involvement in particularly gross immorality, perversion or deviancy. For example there is an abnormally high proportion of individuals involved in incest or prostitution.

A Cure

As I have investigated the topic I have had some questions answered, but I must admit I leave my inquiry with even more questions than when I started. To quote what I consider to be profound words from Sympathy for the Devil, “Please to meet me you hope you guess my name, but what’s puzzling you is the nature of my game.”

There are some facts that I hold more firmly than ever before that I will share in closing. Most every author that I have read, who has investigated this phenomenon, would affirm them. The first is found in Psalm 62, which states, “you, O God, are strong, and that you, O Lord, are loving.” God is real, he is powerful, and he is good-truly good. His goodness and power, particularly over evil, I believe is foundational truth.

Second, there is a very real intelligent power of evil which can not be relegated to a mood, fear, ignorance, psychological projections, altered state of consciousness or simply mental illness. Malachi Martin summarizes that in all of the case studies he examined, the evil spirit, always acknowledged that there was a Prince of evil to whom even they were subservient. By which Martin is saying that none of the exorcisms were direct confrontations with Satan though he believed strongly such a spirit existed.

Third, is clearly the significance of Jesus. In few exorcisms could an evil spirit be brought to say his name. In the true story on which the Exorcist was based the final deliverance of the boy came when he uttered Dominus, which is Latin for Lord or Christ.

I went into ministry believing in the power of the gospel of Jesus Christ. My research into exorcism has affirmed this belief. Here is how I believe it works.
