Ideas on how to use Piper’s Seeing and Savoring Jesus Christ

John Piper’s book Seeing and Savoring Jesus Christ is a wonderful tool for campus ministry.

1. Foremost it helps staff, students, and ministries focus on the person of Christ – his greatness, his glory, his power, his grace. If we do not purposefully keep Christ at the center of our lives and ministries, something else will always illegitimately occupy the center: ministry philosophy, fanciful strategies, or charismatic personalities. These aren’t bad things, but they aren’t Christ.

2. The book is versatile; it works well with unbelievers and believers. Believers find Christ beautiful. Unbelievers are also able to consider his greatness. In the foreword, Piper invites unbelievers and believers alike to “Look at him. Don’t close your eyes and hope for a word of confirmation. Keep your eyes open and fill them with the full portrait of Jesus provided in the Bible.”

3. The short chapters are ideal for use in small groups.

There are probably many ways to utilize Seeing and Savoring in campus ministry. I’ll suggest a few, but I’m sure other ways will be spawned as people try it. Here are some ideas:

1. Add 10-15 minutes of Piper discussion to your existing small groups. You can keep teaching whatever material you are using but take 10-15 minutes at the end of every group to talk about Christ. Make it the grand finale of your group. “Let’s step back a minute and pause to reflect on how great Christ is.” We had our more mature small groups read the chapter in advance of group. The younger small groups actually read the chapter in their group (it takes 5-7 minutes).

2. Use a discussion of a Seeing and Savoring chapter as your entire small group content. Look at various passages and spend time in prayer and worship. Think through how you should respond to the Christ presented in this chapter. If you take a whole lesson to discuss a chapter, you will probably want to use the questions I’ve provided towards the end of the discussion.

3. Use Seeing and Savoring as a devotional guide. Perhaps have your whole small group do the same chapter each week. I think, however, that there is power in reflecting upon Christ in a community.

4. At Duke we did #1 above and then did all our weekly meetings in the Spring around Christ. So, for example, every small group in the ministry would do chapter 5 the week before the weekly meeting that talking about this aspect of Christ, his sovereign power.

Critical to the success of using Seeing and Savoring is questions that both target the heart and are Christ-centered. The enclosed brief paper will explain both of these. I’ve provided questions for each chapter that will hopefully be helpful.

These questions for designed for a 15 minute discussion, so you might need to supplement them if you are planning on a 45 minute discussion. On the other hand, the questions are rich and thought provoking and people have told me that discussions based on these questions often went 30-45 minutes.

If your schedule necessitates that you skip chapters, I’d recommend passing on chapter 9 (a difficult chapter), then chapter 4.
