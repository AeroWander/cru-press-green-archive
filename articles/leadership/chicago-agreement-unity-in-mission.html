Chicago agreement: unity in mission

435 words · 2 min read · Article · For staff & team leaders

In brief

A short 2010 interdenominational agreement ('Chicago agreement: unity in mission') among major U.S. campus ministry organizations (including Campus Crusade for Christ, InterVarsity, Navigators, Chi Alpha, and others) outlining shared principles for collaborating rather than competing on college campuses, building on the 1971 Trail West Agreement. Lists commitments such as not recruiting each other's leaders and lists the founding ministries and their representatives.

Vision & strategic planningMovement building & growth

2010 trail west update

  1. We are all part of Christ’s body.
  2. We do not regard any campus as our exclusive field. We recognize that many students and faculty may be helped through the various appeals and styles of the different organizations.
  3. We will seek to establish relationships and build bridges with our counterparts in other Christian groups on campus. When establishing ministries on new campuses, we will take the initiative to communicate with the leadership of existing groups.
  4. We will speak well of and refrain from criticism of each other’s ministries and members.
  5. We commit to addressing problems on a local, regional or national level by humbly communicating with our counterparts, seeking the Lord together to resolve the issues. i Our work was built on the Trail West Agreement of 1971, in which leaders from several campus ministries met to develop a practical means of settling conflicts between local leaders on a number of campuses across the country.
  6. We affirm the leadership commitments students and faculty have made to each other’s ministries and will not actively recruit them away from those groups. When starting a new campus work, each organization will endeavor to select new leaders, not leaders from other ministries.
  7. We recognize students and faculty have the freedom to choose their involvement with any campus ministry. In general, we will encourage them to select and be involved with one primary organization. As ministries committed to the Gospel of Jesus Christ and united in his mission on college campuses, the following groups listed below met on October 25, 2010 and agreed to teach the staff, volunteers, faculty and student leadership of our organizations the following principles about relationships with other Christian groups. (John 13: 34 & 35)i
  8. We will encourage collaborative efforts on a voluntary basis between our organizations. We are open to share experiences and resources to assist each other with the unique challenges of campus ministry.
