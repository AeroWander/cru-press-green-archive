The Indestructible Joy of Jesus

Piper, Chapter 4

1. In John 15:11 Jesus tells his disciples, “These things I have spoken to you, that my joy may be in you, and that your joy may be full.” As Piper notes, quite clearly Christ’s “joy” isn’t without sorrows or suffering. But clearly the Scriptures present Jesus as full of joy.

Peter, for example, in his sermon in Acts 2 relates Psalm 16 to Christ: “You [the Father] have made known to me [Christ] the paths of life; you [the Father] will make me full of gladness with your presence” (2:28).

How is your heart drawn to a “joy-filled” Christ?

2. Piper writes that salvation is more than forgiveness of sins; it is fellowship with Jesus. So, “if this fellowship is not all-satisfying, there is no great salvation” (p. 41).

To what extent is fellowship with a joy-filled Jesus a part of your Christian experience? Why is this the case?

3. One of the more amazing statements in the Upper Room Discourse of John 13-17 is when Jesus says he desires his joy to be in us that our joy would be made FULL (Jn 15). We want “joy” but we look for it from false sources. John 15 seems to imply that the joy Jesus wants to be ours is actually himself – abiding with him.

How do you think intimacy with a joy-filled Jesus would change your life?
