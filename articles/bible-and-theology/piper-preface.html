Seeing and Savoring Jesus Christ

Piper, Preface

Some titles tell you virtually nothing about the book. The other day my son asked me, “Dad, what is the book To Kill a Mockingbird” about?” “Mmm,” I replied, “I think it is about . . . uh . . . ” I’m sure my eighth grade English teacher would be appalled, but the title sure didn’t help me remember the book.

This book is the opposite. The title tells you everything. SEE Jesus Christ. Look at him. Think about him. Then, in light of what you see, SAVOR Christ. Enjoy him. Treasure him above all. Find satisfaction for your soul.

  1. Tell us about something you really savor. What (who?, where?) is it? How does it affect you? Tell us about the savoring . . . the longing, the sweetness. Be specific.

Now let me ask you a question: Have you ever savored Jesus Christ? Why you supposed this is the case? How does your heart respond to the possibility of “savoring” Jesus Christ?

  1. Piper ends the preface with this sentence: “There is no more important issue in life than seeing Jesus for who he really is and savoring what we see above all else” (p. 17).

Why do you think Piper states that there is nothing more important in life than savoring Jesus Christ? Why savoring (versus obeying or worshipping or loving or whatever)?
