## Rick James

I stood there in the bookstore staring, transfixed on the gold-embossed pentagram on the cover. There it was, The Satanic Bible—embodiment of evil. Strangely it was not evil that scared me, but something good, my free will. I had a terrifying thought “What if I were to recant my faith and call upon Lucifer.” I had no desire to do that mind you, I love Christ, but it was the fact that I could, that it was within my power to throw away heaven, that was utterly terrifying.

The security and comfort of our salvation is often short-lived. Mine was anyway. Following on the heels of my discovery of eternal life came the fear that I could loose it. In all honesty, I thought more about that, than I did about my salvation. In point of fact I became compulsive about it, combing through the Bible in search of verses to quiet my growing anxiety. But any verse about salvation, if mentally appendaged with the clause, “unless I loose it,” not only provides little comfort, it actually heightens anxiety. I had salvation, but without security what peace did that bring me? I was anxious here, now, and felt the eternal state of my soul was still in jeopardy, for in theory I had the latent power to relinquish it. I had a winning lottery ticket—so what—I also had the power to tear it up.

I grant you that I am a mental basket-case, but sometimes it takes a basket-case to press a point. For as the English say, few people ever follow an idea through to its “ruddy end.” Basket-cases do that. And that’s where I was, at the ruddy end, which in walking with God, is never a bad place to be. For nothing precipitates an intervention from the Lord like being at the end of ourselves. God starts where we end.

Well, at this point it would be good to mention that I no longer struggle with this issue, or the security of my salvation (I’ve moved on to be compulsive about other things) and that is due to a few of the following lessons God taught me in that state of desperation.

## A Relationship Not a Contract

Negotiations can be broken off and contracts voided but relationships are different. In theory, I can tell my father that I no longer wish to be in his family but in reality I can never change the fact that I am his son. I can sever our fellowship but not our relationship. In John 1:12,13 it says “Yet to all who received him, to those who believed in his name, he gave the privilege to become children of God — children born not of natural descent, nor of human decision or a husband’s will, but born of God.”

Because I made a decision to place my faith in Christ, I have been born into the family of God. I cannot unborn myself, but only use bad grammar to describe the possibility.

## Not One Thing

Salvation was a gift. There is not one thing we did to earn it. Likewise, there is not one thing we can do to loose it: if there were, our salvation would in some way be based upon our performance—which it’s not.

## God Wants US to Feel Secure

I don’t pretend to think that I am in any way as loving as God. But, vile creature that I am, I would want my child (I have several of them) to enjoy the security of knowing they will always be a part of our family. If I said to my daughter, “You will always be in the family provided you don’t (fill in the blank).” Actually let’s fill in the blank. Let’s say I told her that she would always be a member of the family as long as she never touched the Ranch dressing in the refrigerator. Wouldn’t I have done the exact opposite of giving her security? In fact all I’ve done is make her terrified of Ranch dressing.

The world works on the principle, “I will love and accept you if you do x, y, and z.” A family operates on the opposite principle: “I love and accept you, now in gratitude do x, y, and z.” This is grace-motivated obedience and this is what the gospel is about. For me, putting the concept of my salvation and security into the context of my family has helped me to better understand the security God wants me to have. There have also been some passages of scripture that God has used to affirm my security.

## The Authority of God’s Word

“And this is the testimony: God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Son. He who has the Son has life; he who does not have the Son of God does not have life. I write these things to you who believe in the name of the Son of God so that you may know that you have eternal life.” (1 John 5:11-13)

It’s important that we take God at his word. In this passage the apostle John makes it clear that if we have received Christ, we have eternal life—period. I have had to stand by faith upon these verses and not allow anything to pry them away.

## Salvation Is Also a Process

“Being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.” (Philippians 1:6)

## Does That Help?

“Sort of, but what about…?”

I knew you were going to ask that. What about passages in the scripture that imply that you need to persevere in the faith or you may not have eternal life. Let me give you a strange analogy:

Let’s say I’m a magician. And before your eyes I turn a dog into a man. Nice trick. You of course are naturally a little suspicious. Did I really do it? Well, if in fact this man starts to act like a human than it seems I truly have acquired quite a neat skill. But, if the man begins to clean himself, and lap water with his tongue you would quickly realize it was an illusion. What I had really done was dress a dog up to look like a man. The person’s behavior would demonstrate that they were never actually changed.

A long way to go to make a point—granted—but here it is. The scriptures are clear that the only way to know who is changed (born again) is to look to certain outwards signs of life-change. If these outward signs are never manifest than you have good reason to conclude that person is not a possessor of eternal life. Their actions reveal, not that they lost their salvation, but that they never possessed it.

One final note to ponder: if you’ve trusted Christ, the very fact that you are worried about the security of your salvation is evidence that you have it. A lost person simply doesn’t care.
