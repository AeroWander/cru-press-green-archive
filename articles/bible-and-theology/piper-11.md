The Severity of Jesus Christ

Piper 11

1. In this chapter Piper assembles quite a list of troubling statements from Jesus. For example:

*When a disciple asked him if he could go bury his father Jesus replied, “Follow Me, and allow the dead to buy their own dead.”

*“I say to you, My friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. But I will warn you whom to fear: fear the One who, after He has killed, has authority to cast into hell; yes, I tell you, fear Him!”

*“I tell you, no, but unless you repent, you will all likewise perish.”

*“If anyone comes to Me, and does not hate his own father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, yes, and even his own life, he cannot be my disciple.”

We will not explain or understand all of these statements now. That is not the point. The point is that they hit hard. They ARE troubling to our thinking. Piper says that this is because we are always tempted to make Jesus fit the “spirit” of our generation. For example: we think of love as “soft and tender” so we want our Jesus to express love this way. We think of Jesus one way . . . so these statements trouble us.

All of us have “made” our own Jesus by imparting our expectations or the “spirit of our age” onto him.

If you’re honest, as you’ve made your Jesus what have you made him to be?

Or, put another way, What do these troubling statements about Jesus reveal to you about what is untrue about your “made” Jesus?

2. Piper prays, “Give us a passion for the truth that is stronger than our inborn passion for being praised.”

What aspects of Christ and his teachings have you brushed aside in light of your “passion for being praised”?

3. In this chapter we find an unpredictable Jesus. A Jesus talking of the narrow way. A Jesus warning of destruction – in fact, encouraging followers to self-mutilation to avoid damnation (cf. Mt 5:29-30). A Jesus talking of divisions within families, hell, kindling a fire to cast upon the earth, and evil.

There are two primary ways to respond to this Jesus – 1) questioning him, sizing him up, ignoring certain parts of his teaching, being ashamed of certain parts of his message, modifying what he has said to the spirit of the age, and calling him wrong in your heart. Or, 2) Submit and worship. Listen to Piper’s prayer:

We bow before him and shut our mouths.

We are eager for him to speak – and to speak any way he pleases.

We are the silent learners. He is the sinless teacher.

We put our hands upon our mouths and take our place at his feet.

Do with us as you please, Father.

We are not your judge, nor the judge of how your Son speaks.

Have mercy on us – tough or tender – and lead us to your everlasting joy.

Which of these two options best describes your heart’s response to this radical Jesus? Are you willing to change (if need be)?

Pray something like Piper’s prayer together as a group.
