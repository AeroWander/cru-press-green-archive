Piper 7

293 words · 1 min read · Handout · For small-group leaders

In brief

Small-group discussion questions for chapter 7 of Piper's Seeing and Savoring Jesus Christ, on Jesus enduring a false reputation and unjust accusation. Using examples from John 8 and Mark 2 and Isaiah 53's suffering-servant imagery, questions contrast participants' typical response to being wrongly accused with Christ's silent endurance.

Bible study & interpretationKnowing God & doctrine

The Glorious Poverty of a Bad Reputation

Piper 7

  1. Recount a time when your name was wrongly shamed or accused. What did you think/feel?
  1. At times Jesus responds to those who falsely accuse him. For example:

a. John 8:40. “We are not illegitimate children (like you are Jesus) . . . Jesus said to them “you are unable to hear . . . you belong to your father, the devil” (ouch)

b. Mark 2:6ff. “Why does this fellow (Jesus) talk like that? He’s blaspheming! . . . Jesus said, “Why are you think these things . . . . But that you might know that the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins . . . He said to the paralytic, ‘I tell you, get up, take your mat and go home.’”

But often Jesus silently takes the false accusations.

What does this tell you about the character and heart of Jesus? Contrast his response(s) to being wrongly accused with your responses when wrong accused.

  1. The greatest insult to Christ was the cross – the place of greatest shame. He was naked. Hung as a criminal when innocent. Suspended on the tree he made. Taunted by jeers to save himself (like he couldn’t). Isaiah puts it so powerfully:

“He was oppressed, and he was afflicted, yet he opened not his mouth; like a lamb that is led to slaughter, and like a sheep that is silent before its shearers is silent, so he opened not his mouth.”

Is 53:7

What is uniquely beautiful about Christ at this moment?

Or, put another way,

How is his beauty different at this moment than, say, when he raised Lazarus from the dead? What is beautiful about a God like this?
