It’s not all about you

Piper, Chapter 1

1. What are you starved for? Think about it. Talk about it.

2. Piper argues the “gospel of the modern world” has it tragically wrong. In the midst of this vast and glorious universe, all of us as humans, in an attempt to ease the pain of our starvation(s), work to satiate our hunger with self-devised strategies, by staring at ourselves, by attempting to prop up all that hurts in our lives. Our me-O-centric efforts don’t work.

On the other hand, Piper argues “the healing of the soul begins by restoring the glory of God to its flaming, all-attracting place at the center” of our lives (p. 21)

Do you agree or disagree with Piper – do you think that ultimately your starvation – your hunger – comes down to this: You are starved for the glory of God?

3. But how, one might ask, do we do place the glory of God at the center – and consequently ease our hunger? Paul tells us in 2 Corinthians 4:6:

“God, who said ‘Light shall shine out of darkness,’ is the One who has shone in our hearts to give the Light of the glory of God IN THE FACE OF CHRIST.”

When we look in the face of Christ we see the glory of God.

What (or who) can be found “in the face of Christ” that can heal the aches of your heart and satisfy the hunger in your soul?

Or maybe put another way: What is beautiful about the glory of God in the face of Christ that far surpasses the “beauty” of any self-devised plan to satisfy your hurting heart?

The next few weeks we’ll fill our eyes with the full portrait of Jesus as provided in the Bible – seeing Him for who he is, savoring him for who he is.
