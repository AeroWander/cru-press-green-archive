Hosting a Justice Week

1,859 words · 8 min read · Article · For student leaders

Part of the series Extracts & Documents

In brief

A leader's planning writeup and template for hosting a campus 'Justice Week,' a partnership between IJM and Cru at Colorado State University, aimed at raising awareness of human trafficking. It details goals, a day-by-day schedule (prayer, flash mobs, tables, fundraising, a film night), Soularium table scripts, resource lists, and after-action notes on what worked and what to improve.

Soularium Table-CSU justice week “Thanks for your desire to help engage the CSU students and faculty on the topic of justice. Enjoy your time as you are, for a moment, the voice of those who are enslaved and oppressed around the world. Pray that God will use this hour to raise up an army who will combat the evil in the world and become those who ‘set the captives free’.”

The goals of the table:

  1. 2. 3. To engage our community on the topic of injustice.

To raise awareness of the issue and catalyze help.

To represent the heart of Christ to the campus, as those with no voice are close to His heart.

Please:

  1. 2. 3. 4. 5. Use Soularium cards as a springboard into your conversation. Replace the standard Soularium questions with the following:
    • a) Which image best represents injustice? Why?
    • b) Which image best represents justice? Why?
    • c) Which image best represents God’s view of injustice? Why?

Listen well and limit your speaking.

Speak boldly of your hope in Christ and how He came to bring an end to injustice.

Get contact information from those who visit the table.

What went well: Plaza visuals were VERY helpful.

What didn’t/needs to be changed: Be sure to get good rooms ahead of time. I think it would be beneficial to have I-empathize multiple days instead of just one. Bricks on Campus Provide a visualization of the reality of slave labor.

  • Purchased 50 bricks from an ad on Craigslist for $20. I’d recommend trying to get 5 times as many bricks (or more, 1000 would be awesome!) Maybe someone would let us “borrow” a full pallet and we then just pay for any we break. Worth checking into at Lowe’s, Home Depot, etc.
  • We had sign-ups at FRESHMAN Bible Studies and the Justice Week info meeting for 30 minute time slots. You need at least two people the whole time but we had more for most of it, as many as 6 carrying bricks at once. The more the merrier (and bigger impact)! This was good especially during passing periods so we would be obvious. At some points we had people handing out IJM cards with facts on them and trying to engage people in conversation (this worked better during class times when there were fewer people walking by). It would be cool to bring “passer-bys” into the demonstration and have them join in carrying bricks.
  • Participants carried 1-2 bricks at a time and held them on top of their heads for a better visual. We carried bricks from east to west (and back) across the plaza building one stack and then moving it back to the original location about 30-40 yards away and so on. This way we were crossing through everyone that walked by. Also, it’s cool to have a couple people just stand silent in the middle of traffic holding a stack of bricks while others are carrying bricks around them. Participants also wore white signs that said “I am a SLAVE trapped by bonded labor.” and had IJM.org on the bottom. The signs were on a cardboard backing that hung by metal chains from the neck. They looked pretty cool.
  • Most participants seemed to enjoy the experience and I know for a number of students it helped them to think about what goes on and engage with those forced into slave labor. I would recommend really encouraging participants to think about this and put themselves in a “slaves shoes” while they are carrying the bricks. The task is very monotonous and boring but, in part, that is the point.
  • Idea for next year: pass out bricks to a lot of students and have them carry them in their hands all day long (to classes, lunch, etc), using the brick as a conversation piece to engage people with the idea of justice and what we are doing. Arm them with info cards they could give to interested people. IJM Documentary Show “The End of Slavery” in the student union, multiple times (every hour on the hour). This was a one day event. I would show it from 11am-3pm or later.
