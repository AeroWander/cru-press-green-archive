In brief

A leader's guide describing a Soularium-based small-group icebreaker activity, in which participants use Soularium's photo cards and guided questions to tell the story of their faith journey to the group. It includes discussion prompts, format options for a single session or spread across weeks, and reprints the separate "Training Others" guide for teaching people to use Soularium more broadly.

Soularium™ Team What Is It?

Here’s a constructive icebreaker to do with small groups. It will help people:

  • Connect and get to know each other
  • Reflect on their own faith journey
  • Become comfortable talking about their story with others
  • Find connections between their personal faith stories and the stories of others ASK THE ONE NON-SOULARIUM™ QUESTION:
    1. As you think about your spiritual journey, what Scripture has meant the most to you? Why? INVITE PARTICIPANTS TO SHARE THEIR STORIES Give your group time to think about these questions, select images, and recall a meaningful verse.

What Do I Do?

Here’s a couple options:

Set aside 15 minutes of your group time over several weeks. Each week have one or two people tell their story using the Soularium™ images and their scripture passage. OR, have everyone tell their stories during one meeting. (As the facilitator, you may want to consider sharing your story first.)

INTRODUCE THE QUESTIONS “In a moment, you will be reflecting on four questions. Three of these questions are designed to help you tell the story of your faith journey using Soularium™.” MATERIALS REQUIRED:

Ideally, one Soularium™ deck for every two or three participants, but at minimum provide one. “Consider the following way of thinking about your story. Imagine your life right now as the final scene of a movie. If you were to choose three frames from this movie (known as your life) to summarize what has brought you to where you are spiritually, which three frames would you choose?”

ASK THE THREE SOULARIUM™ QUESTIONS:

  1. Which image represents the turning point in your life when you really began to follow Christ?
  2. Which image represents what led up to that? (What was true of your life before then?)
  3. Things are different after a turning point; which image represents a part of your life you’ve seen change as a result? The Heart of Soularium

Soularium is designed to help us genuinely and sincerely explore and understand another person’s story.

We are confident that the Holy Spirit will open doors to the gospel during many Soularium conversations — perhaps even most. But it’s possible to have a successful Soularium conversation that doesn’t get to the gospel.

So this raises the question, what is a successful Soularium conversation? It’s successful if the user does these 5 things:

  • Initiates a conversation
  • Asks (open-ended) questions
  • Genuinely listens
  • Explores the other person’s life story
  • Follows the leading of the Holy Spirit Enjoy using Soularium! ~The Creators of Soularium

Suppose someone has died and we think that a murder may have been committed. The detective examines the scene and begins a long investigation. She seeks to identify the responsible person by gathering facts at the scene and determining in which direction these facts point. Some of the evidence will point to the fact that the death was caused by some person without identifying who the person is. Other lines of evidence may go far towards identifying the person. Sometimes what first appears to be a promising line of evidence turns out to be a dead end. Other times, some fact that appears to be insignificant at first winds up providing the key.

Subhead Level One

It is unusual for a detective to find a single piece of evidence that will provide absolute certainty about the identity of the criminal. Rather, detectives try to build what can be called a cumulative case. The various lines of evidence work together to make it more and more reasonable to think that a particular suspect is guilty. If the cumulative case is strong enough, the suspect may be convicted of the crime. The evidence needs to establish his identity beyond a reasonable doubt.
