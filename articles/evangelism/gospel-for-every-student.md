## Every Student

## Gfes 2010-11

The Campus Ministry has one strategic focus: Win-Build-Send in the power of the Holy Spirit in order to fulfill our Missional Objectives (i.e., Leaders; Teams; Mobilized Laborers; Gospel Experiences; Changed Lives). This strategy is employed with students and faculty, on campuses, among ethnics and in cities throughout the US and the world. The Gospel for Every Student (GFES) initiatives align our evangelistic resources (tools, tactics, training and money) to serve our Missional Teams as they lead evangelistic movements they lead. The vision is to help provide each person within a team’s scope multiple opportunities to hear, understand and respond to the gospel.

The GFES strategic initiative seeks to gain the greatest leverage and momentum by being: Guided by local level planning and priorities Shaped by field ministry leadership Resourced organizationally

The following is a sketch of our current GFES initiatives & resources, Spring 2010.

## 1. Shaping Leaders Thinking

## The Evangelism Model

GETTING BIBLICAL ABOUT EVANGELISM Built around The Evangelism Model, this study guides the user through 14 key Bible passages, leading to the discovery of evangelism principles about: The Master; The Masses; The Messengers; The Message; and The Methods.

God: Evangelism is first & foremost a work of God. Therefore, a leader must learn to discern and align to what God is doing in their context.

World: The audience is the defining context Therefore, the leader must understand and adapt to the audience in their context.

Believers: Believers are the primary initiators in evangelism. Therefore, the leader must empower, equip and engage the labor force in effective outreach.

Gospel: The true gospel is always about Jesus—who he is, what he has done and why. Therefore, the leader must ensure that the unchanging gospel is communicated with life-changing power.

Methods: Methods will vary according to the context.

Therefore, the leader employs effective methods that bring it all together.

EVANGELISM MODEL: A TRANSFERABLE CONCEPT Available in early 2010, this White Paper will articulate the Evangelism Model in a clear and transferable way. It will serve as a foundational resource for training and development.

## 2. Equipping for Gospel Conversations

## Cojourners

Our bread and butter evangelism continues to be a gospel conversation between an equipped believer and an open student or faculty member. CoJourners is our equipping paradigm to teach believers to conversationally enter the spiritual journey of others, helping them come to Christ. CoJourners teaches evangelism through four primary roles:

The Explorer: Initiating spiritual conversations, the believer discovers the spiritual journeys of others through active listening and asking questions.

The Guide: The believer guides another to faith in Christ through their own life-story and the gospel.

The Builder: There are many issues and obstacles that can hinder those yet to come to Christ that need addressed through prayer and gentle persuasion.

The Mentor: Those who come to Christ need encouragement to continue on their journey through relational connections to Christian community and through foundational concepts for Christian living.

COJOURNERS TRANSFERABLE CONCEPT A 16-page article that explains and teaches the CoJourner approach to evangelism. This simple-to-follow, easy-to-apply article equips students for effective witness.

COJOURNERS DEVOTIONAL A 28-day, evangelism focused, daily devotional, providing biblical motivation and practical steps for effective witness. Devotional thoughts touch on prayer, motivation and each of the four CoJourner roles.

COJOURNERS EQUIPMENT PACK The pack provides 24 brief discussions for equipping small groups in effective witness using the CoJourner roles. These training nuggets are ideal complements to Bible study content, helping small groups develop a healthy outward priority.

COJOURNERS TRAINING NOTES A one-time CoJourner overview and a five-session CoJourner training is available for download, along with powerpoint: www.CoJourners.com

Overview of Backstory: A general introduction (and mini-training) for Backstory:

Backstory is a tool that allows you to communicate God’s story (the Gospel) through dialogue. Think of it as a conversational guide rather than a presentation. Backstory is best used after you’ve had a spiritual conversation using Soularium or other exploring/ dialogue tools.

Generally, read the left side and ask the questions on the right. There is seven and a half minutes of content in the introduction, the black picture pages and the decision/prayer pages. (There is six minutes of content in the same sections of Knowing God Personally.) The opposite white pages in Backstory provide additional conversational questions, explanations and apologetic ties that can be used if and when appropriate.

After you have EXPLORED their spiritual background or experience, you can say something like this: Thanks for sharing your story with me. Would it be OK with you if I shared how I believe God’s story intersects with your story and mine?

If the answer is yes, show them the Backstory booklet and say: “This is called Backstory, and it highlights themes from all of our stories, and shows how our stories connect with the larger story of life.”

(If they don’t have time, you can give them the booklet to read on their own.)

Then turn two pages, and read aloud in its entirety the page that begins: “There are seven billion people in the world.”

1. Read aloud the black pages, beginning with the white text (the summary statements), followed by the gold text (the Bible passages). To make things more interactive, you could ask them to read portions of the text.
2. To facilitate discussion of the content on the black pages, use the questions (Q ) and other content on the opposing white pages. Besides listening well to their answers, feel free to share your answers as well.

It is a conversation after all!

3. Use the gold strip background content sparingly. Encourage them to read this later.
4. When you get to point “07 Reunion,” be sure to cover the “Your Thoughts?” section carefully. It is important for them to consider the questions: “Is it true?” and “Do you need Jesus?” If they believe it is true, and if they express that they need Jesus, then go on to the next page.
5. Read the “End of Story, Or Beginning?” page (except the gold headings) and pause after asking: “Does this prayer express the desire of your heart?” If yes, ask if they’d like to pray the prayer right then (silently or out loud). If not, keep talking / explore why / give them the booklet.

GREEK/STUDENT PLANNERS Planners are most effective when used in conjunction with a training meeting (team meeting) for a residence hall, fraternity meeting, athletic team or group of students from a university club. ## Gospel Conversations

QUEST An interview tool used to initiate spiritual conversations and to answer the question, “What are students thinking?” The primary objectives of QuEST are:

Locally, to enable students to engage others in spiritual conversations, and Nationally, to assist Research & Development in gathering useful information about student outreach and student thought.

There are three versions of the QuEST interview, each starting a spiritual conversation but from slightly different angles. quest.campuscrusadeforchrist.com/

PERSPECTIVE A card based interview, Perspective provides conversational evangelism tool exploring worldview issues. Developed by Barry Warren, this 58-card set provides opportunity to explore a person’s views of God, human nature, Jesus, meaning in life, and the source of spiritual truth. Perspective will be field tested this winter. http://perspective.uscm.org

For additional information contact: Eric.Pederson@ uscm.org or Barry.Warren@uscm.org

SOULARIUM We live in a visual culture. Because images connect deeply with our emotions and experiences they also enable us to engage people in spiritual conversation on a deeper level. Soularium provides 50, 4x6 original photographic images and 5 simple exploring questions that allow the user to engage in one-on-one spiritual conversations with non-believers. The questions explore the desire of students for their spiritual journey and create many bridges to dialogue about Jesus. Permission to use enlarged images on Soularium displays for high traffic areas can be obtained.

Responses can be entered online in a website (mysoularium.com) that allows you (and the people you talk to) to discover what the trends are among students on your campus. The website is also linked to everystudent.com for further spiritual exploration. To order go to www.crupress.com

For additional information contact: soularium@ uscm.org
