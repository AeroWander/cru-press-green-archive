In brief

A 21-day devotional by Carolyn Raye for graduating seniors seeking God's will, structured as daily readings with meditation verses, discussion questions, and reflection prompts on topics such as living for God's glory, eternal versus earthly treasure, contentment, loving others, and keeping an eternal perspective.

Ready for life after college? This is a 21-Day Devotional on seeking God’s will in life decisions.

Ready to Know

Day Nine Living for His glory MEDITATE: “But may it never be that I should boast, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ…” Galatians 6.14 READ: Galatians 6.11-15 (Ephesians 2:8-9)

Do you live by grace? Try this experiment:

Let’s say your morning begins with a great QT and extended prayer time. A person in your class tells you that they have been thinking about spiritual things and wanted to know what you think. How would you feel about sharing your faith?

Now let’s say, you woke up late this morning, missed your QT and had a critical attitude all morning. You go to class and the person asks you about spiritual things. Would you feel differently or less comfortable about sharing your faith? Why?

What does this experiment tell you about whether you live by grace or by your works?

When you talk about ministry, are you more likely to start by speaking of God’s remarkable work in your life and ministry or are you more likely to begin by talking about your-self?

Verse 15 essentially says that it is not what we do or what we do not do that matters, but what matters is what God has done in making us a new creation. How does this give you freedom? How does it warm your heart to the Savior? THINK: To think that God will love you more for what you do, or that you can have a greater standing before God because of what you do, is to boast in your works, not in the cross. Likewise when we think that our gifts, talents, and abilities is what is making our Bible study or ministry successful is also to boast in our flesh. How can you boast in God’s grace in your life and ministry?

Day Ten Laying up treasures in Heaven MEDITATE: “For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also.” Matthew 6:21 READ: Matthew 6:19-24 (Luke 12:16-21)

Where are the two places that you can lay up treasures for yourself? What happens to the treasures in each place? Think of someone you know who has placed heavenly values over earthly values and vice versa. What is it that you see in their lives to let you know where their treasures (and thus, their hearts) lie?

What are some indicators that someone your age is laying up treasures on earth?

What are some indicators that someone your age is laying up treasures in heaven?

THINK: As you think about college graduation, what occupies your dreams and thoughts the most? (Success, significance, making money, gaining possessions, status, prestige, serving God, loving others, making an eternal impact?) Would you say that your thoughts revolve more around storing up earthly treasures or heavenly treasures? What factors have caused you to think this way?

Day Eleven Walking the path to true contentment MEDITATE: “But godliness actually is a means of great gain, when accom-panied by contentment.” 1 Timothy 6:6 READ: 1 Timothy 6:6-19 (2 Timothy 4:7)

What warnings are given about things to avoid in our pursuit of godliness? What reasons are given for avoiding such snares?

What actions help us to ‘fight the good fight of faith’? What attitudes help us to lay a ‘good foundation for the future’? Knowing yourself, what temptations do you need to avoid in order to ‘fight the good fight of faith’?

What principle in this passage, if applied to your life, would cause you to be more content in life? How can you live out that principle in the next few years?

THINK: What is it that you really want out of life? When you think in the category of “things money can’t buy” what are some of the most important things to you? How can you pursue those dreams and desires in each of the career options before you?

Ready to Hear

Day Seven Keeping an eternal perspective MEDITATE: “Since you have been raised with Christ, set your hearts on things above, where Christ is seated at the right hand of God.” Colossians 3:1 READ: Colossians 3:1-4 (Matthew 6:33)

Chapter 3 begins by addressing those who have been raised up with Christ. This means that believers have been brought to life spiritually through Jesus and that in Him many things are true of us. Make a list of all the things that Colossians says is true of you ‘in Him’, ‘through Him’, and ‘with Him’ (read also 3:3). How does this warm your heart to the Savior?

Our response to these things is to set our hearts and minds on things above, not on earthly things. What are the earthly things that tend to capture your heart and/or captivate your mind?

‘Setting your heart’ suggests striving after heavenly things and ‘setting your mind’ suggests concentrating on them. How does concentrating on the things Colossians says is eternally true of you motivate and give you boldness to strive after eternally significant things?

THINK: C.T. Studd said, “If Jesus Christ be God and died for me, then no sacrifice can be too great for me to make for Him.” Verse 4 ends with a reminder that since the ‘in Him’, ‘through Him’ and ‘with Him’ things are true of us, that we will be with Him in glory. Eternal perspective involves seeing yourself as you are seen in heaven and pursuing heavenly things. How would your desires, dreams, and beliefs change if you were to set your heart and mind on these things?

Day Twelve Examining the Greatest Commandments MEDITATE: “By this all men will know that you are My disciples, if you have love for one another.” John 13:35 READ: Mark 12:30-31 (Revelation 3:15-16; John 13:34, 35) How are we to love God? How are we to love others? Why, out of the hundreds of commandments in the Old Testament, do you think that Jesus said these were the two greatest commandments? How do these “sum up the whole law”?

What can compete for your love for God? What can cause you to be “lukewarm” as described in Revelation 3:16? What is an unselfish act that you can do in order to show love to someone else this week?

THINK: These commandments apply to all believers, regardless of their vocation. As you consider the career options before you, ask yourself, ‘how will I be able to love God and love others’ as Jesus commanded. How well will you be able to live out this command? Day Thirteen Praying for workers in the harvest MEDITATE: “The harvest is plentiful, but the workers are few.” Matthew 9:37 READ: Matthew 9:36-38 (John 4:34-35)

What caused Jesus to feel compassion?

What do you think He was “seeing” as He looked at the multitudes?

What was the specific instruction that Jesus gave to the disciples about what they were seeing?

What stirs your heart to feel compassion for other people? When was the last time you had a unique experience of seeing someone through God’s eyes? What did it cause you to do or to feel?

Ready to Hear

Day Six Knowing I am His son/daughter MEDITATE: “Therefore you are no longer a slave, but a son; and if a son, then an heir through God.” Galatians 4:7 READ: Galatians 4:4-7 (Romans 8:12-17)

What are some of the differences in how a slave and a son respond to the head of the house? As you think about obedience to the Lord, do you interact with Him more as a son or a slave?

Our Father deeply desires that we relate to Him as children, who are free to obey and love Him and not as slaves who fearfully labor for one who does not want their hearts, but only their work. How does meditating on your sonship in Christ warm your heart to the Savior?

Have you ever meditated on how big of a deal it is that you are God’s son/daughter? Consider Ephesians 2:1-10. There is a huge difference between who we were before Christ and who we are now. It was not enough for God to save us in our rebellion against Him. He also made us His children.

God also makes us His heirs. What does this mean? What are we heirs of?

THINK: You are a child of the Most High God! You no longer have to live as a slave to sin (because you are saved) nor are you a slave to God, since you are His son. How does knowing you are His son give you security and rest? How does it motivate you for a life of obedience? How would your sonship enable you to make bold decisions about your life?

Day Five Believing that He knows me MEDITATE: “O Lord, Thou has searched me and known me.” Psalm 139:1 READ: Psalm 139:1-16 (Ephesians 2:10)

What does the Psalmist claim that God knows about him? How does he respond to that realization?

What does God know about you?

Will you thank Him now for all that He knows about you, including your past, the path of your life so far, and the way the he made your physical body? (be specific)

THINK: Nothing about your life has escaped God’s notice. The events, circumstances and emotions of your life are all known by God. Are you willing to thank Him for both the good and the bad and allow Him to use those things for His will and His glory no matter what you do after college?

Day Fourteen Knowing God’s Heart for the Nations MEDITATE: “Let the nations be glad and sing for joy…” Psalm 67.4 READ: Psalm 67 (Romans 10:12-16)

Psalm 67 talks a lot about the nations praising God, being glad, and singing for joy. When you think of worshipping the Lord, what things are you most thankful for? John Piper suggests that missions exist because worship of God does not. In other words missions exist to bring worship to people who cannot worship or be glad, for they have no Savior. What would your life be like if you had no cause to worship? If you had no Savior?

How does it make you feel to know that God uses His people as His means to bring worship to the nations? Read Romans 10.12-16. Who brought you to worship the Lord? THINK: God’s heart for missions begins with His desire to be glo-rified throughout the world. God is glorified when we worship Him with our lips, our actions, and our lives and when we take our worship to nations that do not know the name of Jesus. Thus missions is worship. Have you ever thought of missions as an act of worship? What would it be like to see a person in another country who has never heard the name of Jesus worship Him for the very first time? Let the nations be glad!

Day Four Renewing my mind MEDITATE: “Whom do I have in heaven but You? And the earth has nothing I desire besides You.” Psalm 73:25 READ: Psalm 73 (Romans 12:1-2)

How does the Psalmist describe his perspective and attitude (73.1-5; 13-14)? What is his perspective and attitude towards the wicked who surround him (73.6-12)?

Can you think of a time when you have had a crisis of faith (when your foot almost slipped)? What brought you out of it?

The turning point of the Psalm is in vv. 15-17 when the Psalmist meets with God. From this point until the end of the Psalm, his perspective changes and he begins to perceive things rightly, from God’s perspective. How has meeting with the Lord corrected your own perspectives, revealed your sin (vv. 21-22), or changed the way that you perceive the world, the wicked, and yourself?

THINK: God is our creator and sovereign Lord. He is blind to nothing and even when bad things happen, we can have confidence that He is in control. When we see the wicked prosper or other things that make no sense to us, we must take comfort in God’s word and do our best to know His perspective. When you see life played out in front of you, do you interpret it in light of God’s word? When you feel hopeless, where do you turn in God’s word to bring you comfort? As you look towards life after college, do you see it through a Biblical grid?

Holding on to sin not only affects us spiritually, but also physically, emotionally and relationally. How have you seen those affects of sin in your own life? Think of a specific example.

If there is any sin in your life today that is hindering your relationship and communication with God, will you confess it and make things right?

THINK: How would sin—- something you’ve done or an attitude that you’re holding on to—- affect your ability to hear from God as you pray about His will for your life after college? As you seek to have a clean heart before God, consider areas of your life such as lust, selfishness, materialism, greed, unforgiveness, holding grudges, demanding your own way, worry, impurity or immorality, etc. How could sin in one of these areas of your life be hindering you from hearing clearly from God?

Day Nineteen Asking for wisdom “But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God.” James 1:5 READ: James 1:2-8 (Philippians 4:6-7)

What instructions are we given in this passage (our part)? What is true about God in this passage (His part)? Why do you think there is such strong instruction about asking for wisdom and not doubting that God will give it? Describe the difference between ‘praying with confidence’ and ‘praying with doubts’. Which one would best describe your prayers? Why?

What are the specific things that you need wisdom about today? Have you seen Him give you wisdom in the past when you had a big decision before you? Describe that situation.

THINK: When you pray about your future, do you really believe that God will guide you? What are you praying for? Is it specific? Will you know when it has been answered?

Prayer.

A relationship with God involves communication — telling God what is on our hearts, making requests and taking the time to listen and hear back from Him. James 1:5 says that if any of us lacks wisdom, to ask God. Philippians 4:6-7 tells us to let our requests be known. Spending time in prayer causes us to align our will with His will. Do your prayers involve both sincere asking and quiet listening?

Wise Counselors.

Those who have gone before you have learned many valuable lessons. Their insights, if guided by the Holy Spirit, can be of great help in your decision making. Are your ‘counselors’ advising you from a biblical perspective?

Circumstances and Desires.

We often stand amazed at how God works in our circumstances and even changes our heart’s desire. These are powerful factors in making a decision, especially when experienced in conjunction with all the principles listed above. However, circumstances and desires, when isolated from the previous principles, can easily be misleading. Are you giving the proper “weight “ to how circumstances and desires are driving your decisions?

A Process.

The combination of all these things will help lead you in making the BEST decisions. But this is not a fast process, not a onetime prayer or one quiet time. To take all these principles into consideration will require time and patience. Using this guide will help give you some tracks to run on as you seek God for His direction for your life.

Principles for Seeking God’s Will

Master. Mate. Mission. These three major decisions will end up defining most of your life. Once we have established that God is indeed our Master, then the matter of our mate and mission becomes of utmost importance. These two decisions often lead us to asking “what is God’s will” more than any other decisions in life. The following principles and questions will also help you in this process.

Ask the right question.

While it is natural, in our human state and in our American culture, to ask “what is God’s will for me?”, perhaps a better question is “How do I fit into God’s will?” He has a sovereign plan for the whole world. Do you want to fit into His overall plan, or do you want Him to fit into your plans?

A yielded life.

When seeking God’s will, it usually revolves around a specific decision that we are uncertain about. Before we seek God for the future, we need to make sure that we are submitting to God’s will in the present. Is He Lord of your life? Lord of your will? Are you presently doing all the things that you do know to be God’s will?

Guidance from God’s Word.

In the scriptures, we see many ways that God spoke — audibly, visions, revelations, prophets, etc. Today, though we have the full revealed will of God given to us in the scriptures. His word contains principles to guide our whole lives, not just isolated decisions that we will face at various times in life. Are you consistently read-ing and studying God’s Word?

Ready to Act

Day Twenty Giving Him my heart MEDITATE: “But when Cephas came to Antioch, I opposed him to his face, because he stood condemned.” Galatians 2:11 READ: Galatians 2:11-14 (John 12:43)

What comes to your mind when you think about idolatry? Most of the time we think of pagan primitives bowing down to wood or stone idols that they made in their spare time. However, there is much more to the idea of idolatry. One pastor defines idols as, “Anything we believe apart from Christ to make us happy, satisfied, or fulfilled.” Think on this quote for a minute. Write down your immediate thoughts.

Peter used to hang out over meals with his Gentile buddies. But when some of his hotshot Jewish friends came into town, he began to change his lunch plans since he did not want to be seen hanging out with ‘unclean’ Gentiles. At this instant, Peter began to care more for his popularity and approval than the gospel.

Idols arise when we desire something more than we desire Jesus, put our trust in anything other than God, or serve any other thing rather than our Lord. You can see this in Peter’s life, how have you seen this in yours?

THINK: Generally, there are 4 things that can steal our hearts from God. When they do we begin to obey them more than the Lord and make decisions in light of them rather than making decisions in faith. They are: seeking comfort (fear of stress, demands), seeking approval (fear of rejection), seeking control (fear of uncertainty), and seeking power (fear of humiliation). Which do you struggle with? How can you replace it with faith in God?

Ready to Act

Day Twenty-one Being a blessing to others MEDITATE: “God be gracious to us and bless us, and cause His face to shine upon us, that Your way may be known on earth, Your salvation among the nations.” Psalm 67 READ: Psalm 67 Consider all the things that you consider to be the Lord’s blessing in your life. Why do you think that the Lord has blessed you so richly in so many ways?

The first two verses of this psalm are very interesting. The psalmist is asking God’s blessing not for himself, but so he might be a blessing to others! Have you ever prayed like the psalmist that God would bless you so that you would have more to give?

The reason the psalmist asked God to shine His face upon them and give them His favor was so God’s way would be known and salvation would be among the nations. God’s glory and the expansion of God’s kingdom were the focal points of his asking for blessing. When you pray for God’s blessings in your life, how could you begin to pray in a way that focused more on your blessing being for the nations and less for yourself?

THINK: What are the blessings God has given you and how could they be used to expand His glory to the nations? What could you begin to do now to develop a lifetime practice of blessing the nations with the things God gives you? Think of God’s gifts of time, finances, and your talents/abilities. Consider how God’s blessings in these categories could be given to advance His kingdom.

Using This Devotional Guide

While most of you probably really want to know God’s will and want to do what He leads you to do, the process involved can seem so mysterious or vague, that it is just easier to utilize quicker or easier methods of making a decision.

This devotional guide has been written with you in mind to help you prepare your heart in seeking the Lord for His guidance in making your career decisions.

Week One —– Ready to Hear This week’s focus will be on examining your own heart condition. You will be preparing your heart to be able to hear from God.

Week Two —– Ready to Know This week’s focus will be on knowing God’s heart and thinking like God thinks. You will be gaining an eternal perspective to guide you in your decision making.

Week Three — Ready to Act This week’s focus will be on following God in faith and obedience. You will be learning biblical truths about the blessings and consequences of your actions.

Next Step — Ready to Pray After completing this guide, would you ask someone to be your prayer partner and pray with you for the next 30 days about what God wants you to do upon graduation?

Hopefully, you are a ‘changed person’, not the same as the day you entered school as a freshman.
