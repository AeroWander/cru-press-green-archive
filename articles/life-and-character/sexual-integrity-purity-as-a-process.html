Sexual Integrity

You’re entering crunch time (and I’m not talking about finals here). The transition to the “working world” is not easily negotiated. The sexual landscape is littered with land mines, even for those of us who are seeking to honor Christ, and a major life transition often arouses the anxiety, uncertainty, and isolation that for many are triggers for lust and sexual sin.

The smells of spring were intoxicating. The winter rains had come and gone. It was the perfect time for an attack—after the torrential downpours and before the backbreaking work of the harvest. It was the time that the general/king knew from years of hardened experience that he must go on the offensive if he were to carry out the mission of his Creator/King.

Yet he remained at home in the comforts of his palace while sending his men to fight the enemy on his behalf. We aren’t given details of what was going on in his head. Perhaps he believed his faithful commander could handle the job without him. Perhaps he was tired from years of battle—the blood, pain, and death. Perhaps he was simply being lazy. Or perhaps he had made small choices over the preceding weeks that had dulled his God-given conscience about the need to follow the orders of his King.

Because he was king, no one questioned his decision, at least not to his face. He spent his days enjoying the trappings of being rich and powerful. He slept. He ate. He played. He carried out his duties in relative peace and quiet. And he even attempted to worship as was his custom. God, however, seemed distant. Every time he tried to get close, he had a gnawing sense that God desired for him to be in a different place. If at that point he had stopped and been honest with himself, he would have become aware that he was not doing what he had been called and created to do as a representative of the Creator/King on earth. Yawn. The good life, while comfortable, was getting old and boring. He got up from his bed and decided to go to one of his favorite spots—the roof of his palace. He loved spending time there in solitude, away from the hustle and bustle. This was where he poured out his heart to the Lord and where God spoke to his innermost being. On this night, however, there weren’t songs of praise flowing from his heart and lips. He felt bored. He felt tired in a lethargic sort of way. He felt an uncomfort-able aimlessness.

The breath went right out of him. In the darkness, even from the roof, he could tell that the woman he saw bathing was physical beauty incarnate. Something powerful grabbed his heart. The pull was overwhelm-ing. Intoxicated with lust, he schemed of ways that he might “have her.” His mood turned euphoric with the thought that, as king, he could pull it off.

The ancient story spares us the details of their late-night rendezvous. We get the picture. We need not imagine the scene in the palace that night. It is clear, however, that the great king, his passion gone wild, would live with the consequences for the rest of his life. And so would countless others. His lust would ultimately lead to murder and the shame of an entire kingdom. The toll on his life and the lives of so many others would be devastating. What went wrong? How could this giant of a man, courageous, loving, and spiritual, be responsible for such heartache and pain?

This is one of the Bible’s most famous stories. David’s unfettered lust connection with Bathsheba found in 2 Samuel 11—12 remains one of the most shocking tragedies involving one of God’s great heroes. David was the model king of the Old Testament. He was the only one of whom it has been said that he was “a man after [God’s] own heart” (1 Samuel 13:14, NIV). Who can read the Psalms, dripping with spiritual passion and desire, without recognizing the deep intimacy and friend-ship David enjoyed with God? To dissect this story would be time well spent. For now, however, let this episode in biblical history ring in our ears and our hearts. Bottom line: If this kind of shipwreck could happen to a guy like David, it could happen to you or me.

You’re entering crunch time (and I’m not talking about finals here). The transition to the “working world” is not easily negotiated. For most of us, our time as undergrads provides the opportunity to have access to a close-knit Christian community. I’ve found that campus life, though full of outrageous temptations, not the least of which are sexual in nature, fosters a lifestyle where deep relationships with other believers are not unusual. As you enter the marketplace, there will be new and intense challenges. The stresses of life will pull you, sometimes imperceptibly, away from kingdom living. It will take great intention to continue to pursue Christ as your treasure when many around you will be looking for the Promised Land in an assortment of other pastures. As with David (and you can mention other biblical characters like Samson and Solomon here too), the sexual landscape is littered with land mines even for those of us who are seeking to honor Christ. When referring to the topic of sexuality in this article, I mean to encompass our thought life regarding prospective encounters with sexuality: that to which we are commonly exposed in the media (including the Internet) and in our relationships with those of the opposite sex.

My hunch is that I don’t need to sell you on the fact that God treats our sexuality seriously—for many reasons. When we sin sexually, we sin against our own bodies. Sexual sin damages our most important relationships, not the least of which is the marriage bond—a mirror of Christ’s relationship with His bride, the church. Sexual sin often takes on a life of its own and can quickly spin out of control. Sexual sin is a form of idolatry, taking what was created and using it as a substitute for what only God can fill, whether it is in the form of pixels, romance novels, chat rooms, or sexually immoral relationships. Perhaps more than any other, it is an area of life that produces great shame and guilt. When it comes to controlling ourselves sexually, the stakes are high. But let’s stop and catch our breath. I’d like to offer some hope here. It is possible to live in sexual integrity. It is possible to walk the journey of progressive victory over lust and sexual temptation. And even more important is the hope that our sexual temptation can lead us into deeper intimacy with Christ and the opportunity to experience His life-giving presence in significant ways.

 a Fresh Look at an Ancient Principle

God’s Word has much to say about personal and sexual purity (see Psalm 51:10; Proverbs 15:16; Matthew 5:8; 15:16; Mark 7:21; Romans 13:13; 1 Corinthians 6:18; 10:8; 2 Corinthians 6:6; Philippians 1:10; 2:15; 4:8; Colossians 3:5; 1 Timothy 1:5; 4:12; 2 Timothy 2:22; Hebrews 13:4). I’d like to introduce a phrase that I’ve found helpful in creating an environment in which we can experience freedom in the sexual area of our lives. The principle of sexual integrity, while honoring the biblical pursuit of purity, creates a context by which we move toward the Lord in relationship. Here are a couple of examples of how integrity can be defined: “The quality of being honest and morally upright.” “Moral soundness, honesty, freedom from corrupting influence or motive.” Note the emphasis I’ve placed on honesty in the definitions above. We can say that honesty—“walking in the light,” to use biblical language (1 John 1)—is the context through which we move toward sexual integrity. In fact, as you move into the marketplace, the foundation for success in honoring God sexually will be your willingness and intention to be honest and to walk in the light with God and others.

Living in the Light—what It Looks Like

Sexual sin and lust are primarily results of a deficiency in the way we relate in deep ways to God and others. We click on a suggestive website, chat with someone we don’t know online, get hooked into romance novels and TV shows, or let our minds run wild with fantasy because we are look-ing for real connection. While there’s no question that God places moral requirements on His children, at the end of the day we need to think about sin as a relational issue. I reach toward the “drug” of lust or sex because there is a relational need that is going unmet in my life. Think about an alcoholic for a moment. At a certain point he realizes that alcohol is destroying his life. Yet he still is powerless to stop drinking. To tell him that getting drunk is wrong and that he shouldn’t do it won’t help him stop. He needs a real connection with God and others to give him the power to stop drinking.

If our problem with sexual sin is primarily about connecting with God and others, so is the solution. This is why it is so important for us to walk in the light, immersing ourselves in a community of others who are pursuing Christ. The Bible speaks about the importance of friendship and how with it we are stronger than without it (Ecclesiastes 4:9-10). Most importantly, it means that we need to express and even embrace our powerlessness and weakness before God and moment by moment surrender to Him (John 15:5).

It is not bad or wrong to be weak. In fact, the Bible expresses clearly that it is only in our weakness that we can experience the reality of Christ’s power in us (2 Corinthians 12:7-10). Practically, it means that I run to Him when I’m tempted and I run to Him when I fail, just like the sinful woman in Luke 7 and the lost son in Luke 15.

And we will fail. In fact, pursuing perfection can lead to a rigid way of life bent on rule keeping. We need to remember: progress, not perfection.

True Accountability

True accountability friendships are comprised of both truth and grace. Grace is the acceptance we experience as we come into the light with God and others. Truth is both my willingness to be authentic as well as my willingness to receive direction from others.

There is great freedom and acceptance in the shared struggle. On the other hand, in good support groups, there is an element of “tough love”—speaking the truth to one another. Clear direction is available through the 12 steps for those who want to get sober.

Good recovery groups exhibit both of these qualities. Nobody goes to an AA support group, for example, followed by a brass band. People enter meetings for the first time broken and beaten up. It doesn’t matter whether one is an ex-con or the CEO of a Fortune 500 company. Our accountability relationships must embrace both grace and truth. And we need to go further than just tracking or informing of behavior. We need to explore what is going on in our hearts—our emotions, passions, dreams. Ultimately our sexual sin flows from our heart (Mark 7:21).

Deal with Emotions

There is always a context to sexual struggle. Living in sexual integrity means that we must become increasingly aware of our emotions. There are two important reasons for this. First, the way that we process our emotions will determine our progress in living in sexual integrity. Our emotions will be processed somehow. We will either, identify, feel, and express them or they will come out sideways in mostly destructive ways such as anger, fear, resentment, or lust. Let me offer an example. But there is a second and more important reason to actively process feelings. Emotions are the windows into our hearts and minds. This morning I realized that much of the fear and anxiety I was feeling about my deadline had to do with being approved by others. I want desperately for people to think I’m good at what I do. Over the years I’ve based my identity on what I do and not on who God says I am. So my feelings exposed some faulty and sinful beliefs. The temptation here is to quickly run to a purely intellectual solution to this problem. However, renewing my mind more deeply involves moving toward an emotional solution: a relationship with God—through his Word, through worship, through service, and so forth—and through experiencing truth and grace from others.

While heart change is ultimately the goal in pursuing sexual integrity, I must create room for lasting change by protecting myself in the midst of sexual temptation. I must recognize my limits in terms of what I can and cannot do.

For example, hotel rooms and I don’t mix very well because of the temptation I experience in watching lustful content on TV. So, if I travel, I need to make sure I’m either rooming with someone to whom I can be accountable or staying with a friend. If neither of these options is possible, I need to make a commitment to those I’m in a covenant relationship with that when I arrive at the hotel I will immediately disconnect the cable connection and bring it to the front desk. I’ve had some looks of disbelief, to be sure, from hotel attendants, but it’s more than worth it for me to avoid the temptation and to stay true to what the Lord has called me to—the particular reason for the trip. This is true freedom. I accept my limits and in surrender make choices that will help to ensure continued connections with God and others. Of course everybody has different weaknesses and vulnerabilities that can potentially trip them up when it comes to remaining in sexual integrity. It’s important to remember that while we can’t protect ourselves from every temptation, constructing boundaries helps to set the table for heart change.

Be Intentional About Making Sexual Integrity a Priority Integrity a Priority Making Sexual Be Intentional About

The road to living in sexual integrity is not easy. It will take everything you’ve got—not in your own strength, mind you, but in working in partnership with the Lord and others. Finding those whom you can walk through life with in grace and truth will take great intention on your part.

First, I urge you to pray, pray, pray. Ask the Lord to lead you to others who have the same heart to grow and walk in integrity.

Then look, look, look. Find a church where the pastor shares authentically from his life. If the pastor leads from his own weakness, it is more likely you will find those he shepherds who exhibit the same quality. Look for others in Sunday school, in a singles group, in small groups at church, and among believers you meet in your neighborhood or in your work environment who you sense might be willing to walk in grace and truth with you.

Take some risks. Jump in. Don’t be foolish, but as the Spirit leads, be will-ing to be authentic and real with your own life. You may expose yourself to being hurt, but you will also open up the possibility of having truly transformational friendships.

The Time Is Now

Picture yourself eight months from now. You’re out of school. You’re at home. You’re lonely. The closeness of the friendships you enjoyed on campus seems far away. The pull toward medicating with lust is strong. graduate. Let the Lord breathe life into the process as you surrender to Him and solicit His help in being proactive to prepare yourself for the onslaught that will surely come your way.

The time to consider the steps toward sexual integrity can’t begin then. Start now to strategize about what you will do to pursue the Lord in sexual integrity. Meet with a few of your close friends and help each other come up with specific plans that you will implement when you You can do this! It won’t be easy. But part of the wonder of the gospel is that through God’s Spirit, His Word, and His people, you have “every-thing [you] need for life and godliness” (2 Peter 1:3).

  2. What do you believe will be the greatest challenges for you in living in sexual integrity after you graduate? What will be hard, relationally? What might present a challenge in your living situation? At your job?

  5. How are you doing in identifying feelings and expressing your emotions? What are ways that you can grow in this area? Does the example cited in the article make sense? In your small group, talk through some examples in your own life of how identifying emotions can lead to the deeper issues of what you believe about God and yourself.
  6. What, in your mind, does true accountability look like? How can you develop relationships that transform your character into becoming more like Christ? How might you positively help others move toward being who God wants them to be, not just in avoiding sin, but in stepping into who God created them to be?
